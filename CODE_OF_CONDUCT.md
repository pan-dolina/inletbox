# Code of conduct

This is a small project. The rules are short, and they are enforced.

## What is expected

- Be civil. Disagree with the code, the design or the argument, not with the person.
- Assume the other side is competent and acting in good faith until it is clear they are
  not. Most disagreements here are about trade-offs, not about who is right.
- Take the trade-offs seriously. "This breaks a guarantee, here is the case" is a good
  argument. "This is obviously wrong" is not.
- Accept a decision once it is made and explained, even if it is not the one you wanted.
- Remember who else reads the thread: people evaluating whether to trust this code with
  their clients' files.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, or personal, political or identity-based attacks.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing anyone's private information — an address, a workplace, an instance URL, a
  screenshot with a real case in it — without their explicit permission.
- Deliberately wasting maintainers' time: knowingly false reports, scanner output pasted
  with no analysis, or reopening a settled decision without a new argument.
- Posting a live token, a password, a `.env` or a real recipient's address in an issue or
  a pull request. Redact first; if one slips out, say so immediately so it can be rotated.

This applies in issues, pull requests, commit messages, discussions and private reports —
anywhere the project is being worked on, and anywhere someone represents the project.

## Reporting

Report anything that breaks the rules above privately, through
[Security → Report a vulnerability](https://github.com/pan-dolina/inletbox/security/advisories/new).
That form is the project's private channel to the maintainers; say in the first line that
it is a conduct report and not a vulnerability, so it is triaged as one. Do not raise it
in a public thread, and do not report it to the person you are reporting about.

Reports are handled by the maintainer, kept confidential, and answered. If the report
concerns the maintainer, GitHub Support is the escalation path.

## Consequences

Depending on what happened and whether it repeats: a private word, an edited or deleted
comment with a reason given, a locked thread, or a block from the repository. Nobody is
banned as an opening move over something a sentence could fix, and nobody gets an
unlimited number of sentences.
